Speaker notes

1. cover

Hi, we're team Karl. Quick intro: Karl is a campus marketplace for UNC students that lives entirely in text messages. No app. Let me show you why we built it.

2. problem

Selling a mini fridge at UNC usually means posting it in a few different group chats, getting buried, then meeting someone you know nothing about. Buying means scrolling all of them. There's no one place that remembers what you're looking for.

3. oneline

Karl is a campus marketplace you just text. Text a photo to sell. Text what you're looking for to buy. If nothing matches yet, Karl remembers, and texts you the moment someone lists it. Facebook Marketplace makes you search. Karl comes to you.

4. demo

Cue points (2 min):
1. Hook: Students already buy and sell across a dozen group chats. Karl is one text away and remembers what you want.
2. Want: judge texts "need a desk lamp under $20". Karl: nothing yet, i'll text you.
3. Sell: teammate texts a lamp photo. Karl drafts "desk lamp, good condition, $15. post it?" Teammate hearts it.
4. Proactive match: the judge's phone buzzes with the lamp. Point at the "Karl right now" panel on the dashboard lighting up.
5. Relay: seller accepts, judge texts "omw", it arrives as "Judge · desk lamp" with no number.
6. Safety: seller types "venmo me first pls". Karl warns the buyer.
7. Proof: point at the dashboard counters.
8. Close: Next is UNC email verification and move-out week.
Fallback: if live fails, play the backup video.

5. why

Students already live in iMessage, so there is nothing to install and nothing to learn. Because Karl is a conversation, it can text you when your want shows up, which a feed can't do. And when a buyer and seller need to talk, Karl passes messages between their two separate chats with a bold label like "Maya · mini fridge". Neither person ever sees the other's number. Karl only messages people who texted it first, and "stop" always works.

6. tech

Photon Spectrum gives us real iMessage. Gemini reads the photo and drafts the listing, decides what the user wants and calls our tools, and creates 768-dimension embeddings so "need a cheap fridge" can match "mini fridge, $40". Tiger Data, Postgres with pgvector and TimescaleDB, holds listings, wants and matches and powers the live stats. Gemini never writes SQL and never sends messages itself; relay routing is plain code, and every relayed message is safety-scanned first. Server on Railway, dashboard on Vercel.

Fill the three numbers from the live dashboard right before presenting. Since going live at HackNC, [__] people have texted Karl, [__] items have been listed and [__] have sold. Next we add UNC email verification so everyone is a verified student, then launch for move-out week, when the most stuff changes hands. Ratings come after that, then other campuses.
